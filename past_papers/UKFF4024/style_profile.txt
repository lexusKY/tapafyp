Course Code: UKFF4024

1. Common Exam Format
- The exam is divided into two sections: Section A (Compulsory) and Section B (Optional).
- Section A consists of one compulsory question, carrying 40 marks.
- Section B consists of three optional questions, from which candidates must answer any two, with each question carrying 30 marks (total 60 marks for Section B).
- The total marks for the exam are 100.
- The typical exam duration is 3 hours, though one instance of 2.5 hours was noted.
- The paper generally consists of 4 questions across 4-5 printed pages.
- An instruction is often included stating that "marks will be awarded for detailed workings" for calculation questions.

2. Common Question Types
- Calculation-based problems: These are prominent, especially in Section A, involving foreign exchange hedging (forward, money market, options), capital budgeting (NPV, APV), arbitrage, futures/options profit/loss, Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC), and the impact of interest rates and exchange rates on returns.
- Explanatory questions: Require clear definitions, descriptions, and explanations of key multinational finance concepts, theories, and strategies.
- Discussion/Analytical questions: Involve assessing advantages and disadvantages, evaluating effectiveness, analyzing the impact of various factors (e.g., regulatory, tax, political risk), and discussing strategic financial decisions.
- Scenario-based questions: A significant portion of questions, particularly the compulsory Section A question, are presented within a detailed business scenario, requiring the application of theoretical concepts and calculations to a practical context.

3. Common Topics / Repeated Focus
- Foreign Exchange Risk Management: Consistently features transaction exposure hedging (forward, money market, options market), internal hedging strategies (natural hedging, netting), evaluation of hedging techniques (e.g., best technique, break-even analysis), and foreign currency options.
- International Capital Budgeting and Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Includes project evaluation (NPV, APV), strategic motives for FDI, factors influencing FDI decisions (e.g., regulatory environment, taxation, political risk, business risk, currency risk), and financing foreign operations (capital structure, cost of capital for MNCs).
- International Trade Finance: Focuses on the Letter of Credit (LC), including its explanation, justification, procedures, and appropriate usage.
- Exchange Rate Dynamics: Covers concepts such as locational arbitrage, sterilized vs. non-sterilized interventions, the impact of trade-related and economic indicators on exchange rates, Purchasing Power Parity (PPP), and exchange rate pass-through.
- Types of Exposure: Differentiates and explains transaction, operating, and translation exposure, including their impact and associated hedging strategies.
- Cash Management in Multinational Corporations (MNCs).
- The distinction and application of speculation versus hedging in currency risk management.

4. Common Command Words
- Calculate, Explain, Discuss, Evaluate, Analyze, Describe, Determine, Justify, Assess, Formulate, Recommend, State, Illustrate, Advise.

5. Answer Style Expected
- For calculation questions: Detailed, step-by-step workings are explicitly required and contribute to marks, not just the final numerical answer.
- For conceptual and discussion questions: Answers should be clear, concise, and well-structured, providing accurate definitions, logical arguments, and comprehensive explanations.
- Application: Candidates are expected to apply theoretical knowledge directly to the specific scenarios presented in the questions.
- Critical analysis: The ability to evaluate different options, compare strategies, and assess the impacts of various factors is crucial, often supported by relevant examples.
- Comprehensiveness: For higher-mark questions, answers should demonstrate a broad understanding by covering multiple facets of a topic (e.g., various risks, different strategies, multiple considerations).

6. Difficulty Pattern
- Section A is consistently designed to be challenging, featuring a comprehensive, multi-part calculation-based problem on foreign exchange risk hedging. This question typically requires not only calculations but also an evaluation or recommendation of the best hedging technique, sometimes involving break-even analysis.
- Section B presents a mix of quantitative and qualitative questions, with difficulty ranging from moderate to high. Quantitative problems can be intricate, while qualitative discussions demand in-depth knowledge, critical thinking, and strong analytical skills.
- Overall, the course requires a strong foundation in both quantitative problem-solving (particularly in FX hedging) and qualitative analysis and application of multinational finance principles.

7. Coding Question Pattern
- No coding questions are present in any of the provided exam papers. The course focuses on financial analysis and decision-making within a multinational context, not programming.

8. UI / Practical Pattern
- No User Interface (UI) or specific software-based practical questions are included. The practical aspect of the course is addressed through scenario-based calculations and discussions that simulate real-world financial situations and decisions.
